The author describes the Blues and Greens as factions that evolved from circus groups into dangerous political parties by 532 [PDF p. 101]. Justinian ordered the execution of riot leaders, but a botched hanging allowed two criminals to escape into sanctuary, sparking six days of rioting [PDF p. 102]. The factions united under the slogan "Nika," demanding the dismissal of unpopular ministers and the deposition of Justinian [PDF p. 102]. With the city largely undefended due to the Persian War, the mob gained control, burning the Senate House and St. Sophia [PDF p. 103]. The rebels crowned Hypatius as emperor, though he had initially sought refuge with Justinian [PDF p. 103]. Ministers advised the Emperor to flee by sea, but the author notes that Empress Theodora refused to abandon the capital [PDF p. 105]. She delivered a speech arguing that imperial dignity was preferable to exile, quoting that "Empire is the best winding-sheet" [PDF p. 105]. Spurred by her resolve, Justinian ordered a final assault led by Belisarius [PDF p. 105]. Imperial forces breached the Hippodrome gates, routing the rebels who were trapped inside [PDF p. 105]. The author reports that thirty-five thousand men were killed in the suppression of the Nika Sedition [PDF p. 106]. Although the factions continued to riot for fifty years, the author observes that they never again threatened to alter the course of history [PDF p. 106].
