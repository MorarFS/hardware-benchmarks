The Byzantine Circus factions, the Blues and Greens, had grown from sporting groups into political parties that interfered in politics and religion [PDF p. 101]. The author characterizes the Greens as Monophysite partisans of the late Emperor Anastasius and the Blues as orthodox partisans of Justinus [PDF p. 101]. In January 532, serious rioting occurred, leading Justinian to order the execution of rioters from both sides [PDF p. 101]. After four men were beheaded, a bungled hanging attempt allowed two criminals to survive, which triggered a six-day riot [PDF p. 102]. The factions united under the watchword "Nika" to demand the deposition of officials and eventually sought to install Hypatius as Caesar [PDF p. 102].

As fires destroyed much of the city, including St. Sophia, the rebels crowned Hypatius in the Hippodrome [PDF p. 103]. While ministers urged Justinian to flee by sea, the author notes that Empress Theodora rose to the occasion by refusing to leave and urging a final assault [PDF p. 105]. Spurred by her words, Belisarius led an attack on the Hippodrome, routing the rebels and killing an estimated 35,000 men [PDF p. 105]. The author observes that while the factions continued to riot for fifty years, they never again threatened to change history as they did in 532 [PDF p. 106].
