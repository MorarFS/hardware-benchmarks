The text introduces the Blues and Greens as powerful Byzantine circus factions that evolved into political parties with religious and dynastic allegiances, posing a serious danger to the state due to their riotous nature [PDF p. 101]. In January 532, Justinian ordered the execution of riot leaders from both sides, but a bungled hanging of two criminals allowed them to escape into sanctuary, sparking six days of desperate rioting [PDF p. 102]. The united factions demanded the dismissal of unpopular officials and eventually sought to crown Hypatius, nephew of the late Emperor Anastasius, as Caesar [PDF p. 102]. With the city largely devoid of troops due to the Persian War, the rebels set fire to major buildings, including the original St. Sophia cathedral, and installed Hypatius in the Hippodrome [PDF p. 103]. Inside the palace, ministers urged flight, but the author notes that Empress Theodora refused to flee and delivered a bold speech urging her husband to fight, declaring that death was preferable to exile [PDF p. 105]. Spurred by her words, Justinian ordered Belisarius to lead a final assault on the Hippodrome, where the rebels were trapped and slaughtered, with the author reporting that 35,000 men were slain [PDF p. 106]. Although this massacre ended the immediate crisis, the author observes that the factions continued to riot for fifty years, though they never again threatened the empire so severely [PDF p. 106].
