H4-Q1: Which ruler reunited the surviving Ottoman realm after the civil wars?
The passage identifies Mohammed, the youngest son of Bayezid, as the ruler who united the remnants of the Ottoman realm. The text states, "Mohammed, the youngest of the sons of Bayezid, was a man of genius : he triumphed over the last of his brothers, and united all the remnants of the Ottoman realm that remained" [PDF p. 362].

H4-Q2: Why did Manuel’s support for the two Mustaphas lead to war with Murad?
Manuel's support for the claimants led to war because the Sultan viewed this action as an attempt to cause trouble against him. The text notes, "He tried to make trouble for Murad, by supporting against him two claimants to the Ottoman Sultanate... This drew down on the empire the fate which had been delayed since 1370: the Sultan declared war on Manuel" [PDF p. 363].

H4-Q3: What immediate development caused Murad to lift the siege?
The siege was lifted because the pretender Mustapha, whom Manuel had supported, began causing trouble in Asia Minor. The text explains, "At last the pretender Mustapha... began to stir up such trouble in Asia Minor, that the Sultan determined to raise the siege and march against him" [PDF p. 365].

H4-Q4: What role does the passage assign to the vision of the Virgin?
The passage assigns the vision of the Virgin the role of encouraging the Greeks to resist the siege with greater spirit than expected. The text states, "The report of a miraculous vision of the Virgin... encouraged the Greeks to resist with a better spirit than might have been expected" [PDF p. 365].
