**Summary**

The passage describes the "Blues" and "Greens" factions, which by 532 had evolved from circus groups into political parties with religious affiliations, posing a serious threat to the state [PDF p. 101]. In January of that year, rioting occurred; Justinian ordered leaders from both sides executed, but bungled hangings allowed two rioters to escape, triggering six days of desperate rioting [PDF p. 102]. The factions united under the cry "Nika," demanding the removal of officials and eventually the deposition of Justinian in favor of Hypatius [PDF p. 102]. With troops depleted by the Persian War, the Emperor relied on Belisarius, who faced stout resistance; fighting around the Augustaeum led to fires that destroyed St. Sophia and other buildings [PDF p. 103]. Hypatius was crowned in the Hippodrome, while Justinian considered fleeing, but Theodora’s speech, preserved by Procopius, urged him to fight rather than accept exile [PDF p. 105]. Belisarius routed the rebels at the Hippodrome, killing thousands; the author notes it is curious that such slaughter did not crush the factions, which continued rioting for fifty years, though they never again threatened history as in 532 [PDF p. 106].
