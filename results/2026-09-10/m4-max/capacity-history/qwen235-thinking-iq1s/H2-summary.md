The passage details the decline of Latin's influence in Eastern regions following the division of the Roman Empire. By the seventh century, Latin-speaking populations east of the Adriatic had significantly diminished, as noted by [PDF p. 169]: "By the seventh century this Roman element was rapidly vanishing." Despite formal titles persisting, Latin became increasingly uncommon even among educated individuals, with [PDF p. 169] stating "already in the sixth century a knowledge of Latin was growing unusual." The author attributes this shift to the practical dissolution of continuity between Rome and the East after Odoacer became King of Italy in 476, compounded by barbarian incursions that decimated Thracian, Illyrian, and Moesian populations. By 620 CE, [PDF p. 170] observes "not a tenth" spoke Latin, leaving only Slavonic principalities and Dalmatian seaports preserving Latin traces.
