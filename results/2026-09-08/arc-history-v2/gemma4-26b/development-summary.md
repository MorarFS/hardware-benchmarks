The Byzantine Circus factions, the Blues and Greens, had grown from sporting groups into political parties that interfered in politics and religion [PDF p. 101]. The author characterizes the Greens as Monophysite partisans of the late Emperor Anastasius and the Blues as orthodox partisans of Justinus [PDF p. 101]. The author describes these groups as being dominated by rougher elements prone to mischief and a serious danger to the State [PDF p. 101].

In January 532, rioting began after Justinian ordered the execution of rioters from both sides [PDF p. 101]. During the executions, a bungled hanging and a slipping rope allowed criminals to escape into a monastery, an incident the author calls exciting [PDF p. 102]. This triggered six days of Nika rioting, where the factions united to demand the dismissal of officials and the installation of Hypatius as Caesar [PDF p. 102]. As fires destroyed much of the city, including St. Sophia, the rebels crowned Hypatius in the Hippodrome [PDF p. 103]. While ministers urged Justinian to flee, the author notes that Empress Theodora rose to the level of the occasion by refusing to leave [PDF p. 105]. Spurred by her words, Belisarius led an assault that routed the rebels, resulting in the reported slaughter of 35,000 men [PDF p. 105, 106].
