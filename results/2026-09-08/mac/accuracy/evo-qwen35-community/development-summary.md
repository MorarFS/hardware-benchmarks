The author describes the escalating political power of the Blues and Greens factions, noting their transition from circus groups to dangerous political parties [PDF p. 101]. In January 532, riots erupted after an execution botch allowed two criminals to escape into sanctuary [PDF p. 102]. This incident triggered six days of desperate rioting where the factions united under the slogan "Nika" to demand the dismissal of unpopular officials [PDF p. 102]. The mob refused to disperse even after Justinian promised compliance, and partisans of the late Emperor Anastasius began shouting for Justinian’s deposition [PDF p. 102]. With the city nearly empty of troops due to the Persian War, the Emperor relied on a small force of guards and Belisarius’s cavalry [PDF p. 103]. Belisarius attempted to clear the streets, but the rebels set fire to the Senate House and St. Sophia, burning much of the city [PDF p. 103]. The insurgents installed Hypatius as emperor in the Hippodrome, though he had initially sought refuge with Justinian [PDF p. 103]. During a council to discuss fleeing, Empress Theodora refused to abandon the capital, arguing that empire is the best winding sheet [PDF p. 105]. Spurred by her resolve, Justinian ordered a final assault [PDF p. 105]. Belisarius attacked the Hippodrome’s entrances, routing the rebels who were trapped inside [PDF p. 105]. The author states that approximately 35,000 men were slain in this sedition [PDF p. 106]. Although the factions continued to riot for fifty years, the author evaluates this event as their closest attempt to change the course of history [PDF p. 106].
