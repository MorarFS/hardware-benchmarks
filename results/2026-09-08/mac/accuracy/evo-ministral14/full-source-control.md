Though the Goths fail to capture Constantinople, the empire’s military strength is permanently weakened, foreshadowing its decline.

However, it also highlights the rise of ascetic monasticism, which sapped the empire’s strength by diverting men from civic duty. Paganism faded, but indifference replaced it, leaving the empire vulnerable to barbarian invasions.

The Byzantine army, though still formidable, faced minor threats from Bulgarians and Saracens, while the empire’s cultural revival lagged behind its military decline. Constantine’s obsession with court ceremonies reveals a ruler more concerned with pageantry than governance.

Emperor John VIII Paleologus, the last ruler, attempts to secure Western aid by converting to Catholicism, but his efforts fail. The empire, already in ruins, collapses under Ottoman pressure, leaving behind a shadow of its former grandeur. The narrative underscores the empire’s irrelevance by the 15th century, its military weakness, and its inability to adapt to changing geopolitical realities.
