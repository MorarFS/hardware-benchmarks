The Byzantine Circus factions, the Blues and Greens, had grown from sporting groups into political parties that interfered in politics and religion [PDF p. 101]. The author characterizes the Greens as Monophysite partisans of the late Emperor Anastasius and the Blues as orthodox partisans of Justinus [PDF p. 101]. The author further describes these groups as being dominated by rougher elements prone to mischief and a serious danger to the State [PDF p. 101].

In January 532, rioting began after Justinian ordered the execution of rioters from both sides [PDF p. 101]. Following a botched execution where criminals survived hanging, a mob broke loose and the city entered six days of desperate rioting [PDF p. 102]. The factions united under the watchword "Nika" to demand the deposition of officials and eventually sought to install Hypatius as Caesar [PDF p. 102]. During the conflict, fires destroyed the Senate House and St. Sophia [PDF p. 103]. While ministers urged Justinian to flee by sea, the author notes that Empress Theodora rose to the occasion by refusing to leave and urging a final assault [PDF p. 105]. Belisarius subsequently routed the rebels in the Hippodrome, resulting in the reported slaughter of 35,000 men [PDF p. 105, 106].
