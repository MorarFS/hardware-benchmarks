The author characterizes the eighty years following Basil the Macedonian’s death as the most uneventful period in imperial history [PDF p. 241]. The author argues that the Byzantine Empire ceased to be a vital bulwark for Christendom after 1370, asserting that its prolonged existence resulted from the Paleologi’s subservience to the Turks [PDF p. 358]. Consequently, the empire remained in a state of exhaustion and debility until its final collapse [PDF p. 367].
