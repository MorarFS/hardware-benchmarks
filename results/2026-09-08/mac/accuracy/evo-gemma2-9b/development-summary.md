The excerpt details the Nika riots of 532 AD, a major threat to Emperor Justinian's rule.  The riots were sparked by the execution of seven rioters, four Blues and three Greens, who had been involved in street disturbances. [PDF p. 102] The bungled executions led to a six-day uprising, with the Blues and Greens uniting against the government. [PDF p. 102] The rioters demanded the removal of unpopular officials and even sought to replace Justinian with Hypatius, a relative of the previous emperor. [PDF p. 103]

The city was largely undefended, as the garrison had been sent to fight the Persian War. [PDF p. 103]  The rioters set fire to several important buildings, including the Senate House and St. Sophia Cathedral. [PDF p. 103]  The rioters installed Hypatius as their leader, crowning him in the Hippodrome. [PDF p. 104]

Justinian's advisors urged him to flee, but Empress Theodora convinced him to fight. [PDF p. 105]  She famously declared, "Empire is the best winding-sheet," arguing that death in battle was preferable to losing his throne. [PDF p. 105]  General Belisarius led a successful counterattack, routing the rebels and killing an estimated 35,000 of them. [PDF p. 106]  Despite this victory, the Blues and Greens continued to riot sporadically for the next fifty years. [PDF p. 106]
