In 532, the Byzantine Circus factions, the Blues and Greens, transitioned from sporting groups into political parties that the author describes as a serious danger to the State [PDF p. 101]. The Blues acted as orthodox partisans of Justinus, while the Greens supported the house of Anastasius and Monophysitism [PDF p. 101]. Following the execution of several rioters, a botched hanging allowed two criminals to seek sanctuary, an incident the author identifies as the commencement of six days of desperate rioting [PDF p. 102]. The factions united under the cry of "Nika" to demand the deposition of officials and sought to install Hypatius as Caesar [PDF p. 102]. During the conflict, rebels burned the Senate House and St. Sophia [PDF p. 103]. Although Hypatius initially sought refuge with the Emperor, he was eventually crowned in the Hippodrome by his adherents [PDF p. 103]. Amidst palace dismay, the author evaluates Empress Theodora as rising to the level of the occasion when she refused to flee and urged a final assault [PDF p. 105]. Spurred by her words, Belisarius led an attack on the Hippodrome, routing the rebels and resulting in the reported slaughter of 35,000 men [PDF p. 105, 106].
