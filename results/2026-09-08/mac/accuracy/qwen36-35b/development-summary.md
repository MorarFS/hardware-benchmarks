The author describes the Blues and Greens as factions that evolved from circus groups into dangerous political parties by 532 [PDF p. 101]. Tensions escalated when Justinian ordered the execution of riot leaders, but a botched hanging allowed two criminals to escape into sanctuary [PDF p. 102]. This incident triggered six days of rioting where the factions united under the slogan "Nika" to demand the dismissal of unpopular ministers [PDF p. 102]. The mob eventually turned against Justinian, installing Hypatius as emperor while the city remained largely undefended [PDF p. 102]. During the chaos, rebels set fire to the Senate House, and the conflagration destroyed St. Sophia and other buildings [PDF p. 103]. Although Hypatius initially sought refuge with the Emperor, he was eventually forced into the Hippodrome and crowned [PDF p. 103]. Inside the palace, ministers urged Justinian to flee, but the author notes that Empress Theodora refused to abandon her position [PDF p. 105]. She delivered a speech arguing that imperial dignity was preferable to exile, a judgment the author presents as decisive [PDF p. 105]. Spurred by her resolve, Justinian ordered Belisarius to attack the Hippodrome [PDF p. 105]. The imperial forces breached the gates and slaughtered the rebels, killing approximately 35,000 people [PDF p. 106]. The author observes that while the factions continued to riot for fifty years, they never again threatened the regime with such severity [PDF p. 106].
