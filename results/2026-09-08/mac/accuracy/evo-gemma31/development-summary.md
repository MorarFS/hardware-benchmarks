The author describes the Blues and Greens as Byzantine Circus factions that grew into political parties [PDF p. 101]. The author evaluates these groups as being prone to mischief and posing a serious danger to the State [PDF p. 101]. In January 532, Justinian ordered the execution of rioters from both factions [PDF p. 101]. When a botched hanging left two men alive, a mob rescued them, sparking six days of rioting [PDF p. 102].

The factions united under the watchword Nika to demand the removal of Eudemius and John of Cappadocia [PDF p. 102]. The unrest escalated into a bid to replace Justinian with Hypatius [PDF p. 102]. Fighting led by Belisarius resulted in fires that destroyed the Senate House and St. Sophia [PDF p. 103]. While ministers urged Justinian to flee, the author claims Empress Theodora rose to the occasion by urging her husband to fight [PDF p. 105]. Spurred by her words, Justinian ordered a final assault [PDF p. 105]. Belisarius routed the rebels in the Hippodrome, where it is said 35,000 men died [PDF p. 105-106]. The author notes that the factions continued rioting for fifty years [PDF p. 106].
