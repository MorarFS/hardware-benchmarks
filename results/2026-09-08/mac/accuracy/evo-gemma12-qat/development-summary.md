In 532, the Byzantine Empire faced a severe threat from the "Blues" and "Greens," which the author describes as circus factions that had evolved into political parties [PDF p. 101]. The author notes that these groups were prone to mischief and posed a serious danger to the State [PDF p. 101]. Rioting began in January when Justinian ordered the execution of several leaders, but a bungled hanging of three rioters caused a mob to break loose and seize the criminals [PDF p. 102]. This sparked six days of desperate rioting where the factions united under the watchword "Nika" to demand the deposition of the finance minister and the city prefect [PDF p. 102]. The author reports that the rebels sought to crown Hypatius as Caesar [PDF p. 102]. While ministers urged Justinian to flee by sea, Empress Theodora refused and argued that death was better than exile [PDF p. 105]. Spurred by her words, Justinian ordered a final assault by Belisarius [PDF p. 105]. The rebels were routed in the Hippodrome and slaughtered in thousands [PDF p. 105]. The author observes that while 35,000 men were slain, the factions continued to riot for fifty years [PDF p. 106].
