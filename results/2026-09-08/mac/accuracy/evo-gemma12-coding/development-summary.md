The author describes the Blues and Greens as circus factions that grew into political parties with a rough, riotous character [PDF p. 101]. In January 532, street rioting led Justinian to order the execution of seven leaders; a botched hanging of three rebels allowed the mob to seize two survivors and declare them sanctuary [PDF p. 101-102]. This sparked six days of desperate rioting under the watchword Nika, with the mob demanding the removal of unpopular officials and the crowning of Hypatius [PDF p. 102]. The rebels set fire to the Senate House and St. Sophia, while the emperor remained in the palace [PDF p. 102-103]. On the sixth day, the mob crowned Hypatius in the Hippodrome [PDF p. 103]. While ministers urged Justinian to flee, the author highlights Empress Theodora’s bold refusal to flee and her insistence that death is better than exile [PDF p. 105]. Following her words, Belisarius routed the rebels at the Hippodrome, where thousands were slain [PDF p. 105-106]. The author notes that despite this slaughter, the factions continued rioting for fifty years [PDF p. 106].
