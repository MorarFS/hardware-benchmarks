Record B: LARCH-92  

The text discusses the political and social unrest in Constantinople during the year 532, focusing on the conflict between the Blues and Greens, two major factions in the Byzantine Circus. These factions had evolved from mere sporting groups into political parties, with the Blues supporting the house of Justinus and orthodoxy, while the Greens aligned with the late Emperor Anastasius and Monophysitism. The unrest began with the execution of rioters, which sparked six days of violent riots. The mobs, supported by partisans of the late emperor, demanded the deposition of Justinian and his officials, leading to a near-revolution. The text notes the involvement of the Imperial Guard and German auxiliaries in quelling the disturbances, but the city was largely defenseless due to the garrison being deployed to the Persian War. The passage highlights the instability and the threat posed by these factions to the Emperor's rule. [PDF p. 101-102]
