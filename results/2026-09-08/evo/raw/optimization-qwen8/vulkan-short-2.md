In the year 532, the Byzantine Empire faced a severe crisis as the Blues and Greens, once mere circus factions, emerged as powerful political forces. These groups, rooted in the city’s chariot racing traditions, had grown into influential parties with strong ideological and religious divisions. The Blues supported the house of Justinus and upheld strict orthodoxy, while the Greens aligned with the late Emperor Anastasius and favored Monophysitism. As the Persian War concluded, tensions in Constantinople reached a boiling point.

Rioting began in January, sparked by the execution of seven rioters, including three who were improperly hanged. The botched executions incited a mob that overran the guards and stormed the city. Over the next six days, the Blues and Greens united under the slogan *Nzka* (“conquer”), demanding the removal of the unpopular finance minister John of Cappadocia and Praefect Eudemius. The unrest escalated as partisans of the late Emperor Anastasius began to call for Justinian’s deposition and the installation of Hypatius, Anastasius’ nephew, as Caesar.

Justinian, weakened by the absence of troops due to the Persian War, relied on a small force of 4,000 Imperial Guards, German auxiliaries, and a regiment of soldiers. The city, nearly defenseless, descended into chaos. The author notes that while the riots were a serious threat to Justinian’s rule, the exact motivations and extent of the mob’s support for Hypatius remain uncertain. The events underscore the volatility of the empire’s political landscape and the growing influence of the Blues and Greens in shaping imperial affairs.
