In the year 532, the Byzantine Empire faced a crisis that threatened both the life of Emperor Justinian and the stability of his rule. The "Blues" and "Greens," two powerful factions rooted in the Circus games, had grown into formidable political forces. The Blues, associated with the house of Justinus and orthodox Christianity, and the Greens, linked to the late Emperor Anastasius and Monophysitism, had increasingly influenced both politics and religious debates. Their rivalry, once confined to the arena, now spilled into the streets.

The unrest began in January when riots erupted, partly due to the execution of seven rioters, including both Blues and Greens. The botched executions, which left some criminals alive, sparked outrage and led to six days of violent rioting. The factions united under the slogan "Nzka," meaning "conquer," and demanded the removal of the unpopular finance minister, John of Cappadocia, and Eudemius, the city prefect. The mob, supported by partisans of the late Emperor Anastasius, even called for Justinian’s deposition in favor of Hypatius, his nephew.

Justinian, facing a city almost devoid of troops due to the Persian War, relied on only 4,000 Imperial Guards, a few German auxiliaries, and a regiment of troops. The situation was dire, and the emperor’s promise to dismiss officials only fueled further unrest. This period of turmoil marked a significant challenge to Justinian’s authority, highlighting the deep divisions within Byzantine society and the volatile nature of the factions that shaped its political landscape.
