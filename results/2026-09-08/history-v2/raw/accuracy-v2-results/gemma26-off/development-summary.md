By 530, the Byzantine Circus factions, the Blues and Greens, had evolved from sporting groups into political parties that interfered in religious and political matters [PDF p. 101]. The author characterizes the Greens as Monophysite partisans of the late Emperor Anastasius and the Blues as orthodox supporters of Justinus [PDF p. 101]. In January 532, serious rioting occurred, leading Justinian to order the execution of rioters [PDF p. 101]. After a botched hanging where two criminals survived, a mob broke loose and the rioters sought sanctuary in a monastery [PDF p. 102]. This event initiated six days of desperate rioting under the watchword "Nika" [PDF p. 102]. The mob demanded the dismissal of officials and sought to install Hypatius as Caesar [PDF p. 102]. During the conflict, rebels burned the Senate House and St. Sophia [PDF p. 103]. Although Hypatius initially sought refuge with the Emperor, he was eventually driven out and crowned in the Hippodrome [PDF p. 103]. Amidst palace dismay, the author evaluates Empress Theodora as rising to the occasion by refusing to flee and urging a final assault [PDF p. 105]. Spurred by her words, Belisarius led an attack on the Hippodrome, routing the rebels and killing an estimated 35,000 men [PDF p. 105].
