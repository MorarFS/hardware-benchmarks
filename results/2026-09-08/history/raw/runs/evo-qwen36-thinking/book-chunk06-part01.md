The empire maintained its economic strength through a strict commercial monopoly [PDF p. 251]. This system funneled Eastern goods through Constantinopolitan warehouses before export [PDF p. 251]. Such trade dominance sustained heavy taxation without permanent damage [PDF p. 251]. Military fortunes shifted after the Caliphate fragmented into competing emirates [PDF p. 252]. The Byzantines recognized an unprecedented opportunity to reclaim eastern territories [PDF p. 253]. Emperor Romanus II appointed Nicephorus Phocas to command eastern forces [PDF p. 254]. Phocas successfully recaptured Crete in 961 after destroying Saracen naval bases [PDF p. 254]. He subsequently advanced into Syria and captured Aleppo in 962 [PDF p. 255]. His military success earned him the throne after marrying Empress Theophano [PDF p. 255].
