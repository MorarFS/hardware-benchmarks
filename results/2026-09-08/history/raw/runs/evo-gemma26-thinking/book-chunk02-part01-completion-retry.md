The Hippodrome contained several important monuments [PDF p. 51]. These included a bronze column and a golden tripod [PDF p. 51]. Constantine repurposed a porphyry column with a bronze image [PDF p. 51]. He built an early, basilica-shaped St. Sophia [PDF p. 53]. A wooden gallery connected the church to the palace [PDF p. 53]. Constantine chose Byzantium as his capital in 328 A.D. [PDF p. 54]. The city was consecrated on May 11, 330 A.D. [PDF p. 54]. He attracted diverse populations through privileges and corn distributions [PDF p. 55]. The name New Rome was used mainly in poetry and rhetoric [PDF p. 56].

Constantine died on May 22, 337 A.D. [PDF p. 57]. His successors faced civil wars and barbarian threats [PDF p. 57]. The Goths had settled peacefully near the Danube [PDF p. 60]. However, the Huns attacked the Goths around 372 A.D. [PDF p. 61]. This forced the Visigoths to request entry into Roman territory [PDF p. 62]. Emperor Valens allowed them to settle as subject-allies [PDF p. 63]. Roman officials then extorted food from the starving Goths [PDF p. 63]. Fritigern led a revolt against these corrupt officials [PDF p. 64]. Gothic cavalry proved decisive at the Battle of Adrianople [PDF p. 65, 66]. The resulting defeat decimated the Eastern Roman legions [PDF p. 67].

Theodosius attempted to stabilize the empire through peace treaties [PDF p. 68]. He enlisted Gothic war-bands to serve as imperial auxiliaries [PDF p. 69]. The author argues this experiment was dangerous for the empire [PDF p. 69]. After Theodosius died in 395 A.D., factional wars began [PDF p. 70]. His sons Arcadius and Honorius were both weak rulers [PDF p. 70]. Alaric led the Visigoths into Italy to seek plunder [PDF p. 75]. Meanwhile, the Gothic leader Gainas fell in Constantinople [PDF p. 77]. In 488 A.D., Theodoric conquered Italy for Emperor Zeno [PDF p. 90]. The East survived because of the character of its rulers [PDF p. 86].

Justinus took the throne in 518 A.D. [PDF p. 91]. His nephew, Justinian, became his successor [PDF p. 92]. Justinian married the actress Theodora in 526 A.D. [PDF p. 93]. Theodora was an intelligent and highly influential councillor [PDF p. 94]. Justinian aimed to reconquer the lost Western provinces [PDF p. 99]. He also fought an indecisive war with the Persians [PDF p. 100]. The general Belisarius proved to be a vital asset [PDF p. 100]. Justinian managed a vast revenue through intense taxation [PDF p. 98, 99]. The author compares his greatness to Louis XIV [PDF p. 95].

The text contains legends and several unresolved questions [PDF p. 51, 82]. For example, the cause of Eudocia's exile remains unknown [PDF p. 82]. Some accounts of Theodora's early life may be libellous [PDF p. 93].
