C. W. C. Oman notes that earlier historians viewed Byzantium as decadent [PDF p. 13]. He credits Finlay and Bury with revising this perception [PDF p. 13]. Megarian colonists founded the city at the Golden Horn [PDF p. 27]. They chose the site following an oracle from Delphi [PDF p. 29]. The location provided a strategic military and commercial advantage [PDF p. 30]. It effectively controlled the trade of the Black Sea [PDF p. 30]. The city survived various Persian and Athenian conflicts [PDF p. 31-32]. Some ancient writers describe the citizens as gluttonous [PDF p. 33]. Byzantium later enjoyed status as a free Roman city [PDF p. 34]. Vespasian later stripped the city of these privileges [PDF p. 34]. Severus caused the city's destruction in A.D. 196 [PDF p. 35]. The city later functioned as a vital fortress for Licinius [PDF p. 37]. Constantine eventually won the city in A.D. 323 [PDF p. 38].

Constantine required a more effective administrative and military center [PDF p. 42]. He rejected sites like Nicomedia and Ilium [PDF p. 43]. He eventually selected the old Megarian city [PDF p. 43]. Legends suggest he traced the city limits with a spear [PDF p. 44]. The city plan included seven surrounding hills [PDF p. 44]. The Milion served as a central distance point [PDF p. 45]. The imperial palace was situated in the south-east [PDF p. 45]. A large forum known as the Augustaeum sat centrally [PDF p. 45]. The Senate House was modeled to resemble Rome [PDF p. 47]. The Patriarch's Palace was near the imperial residence [PDF p. 47]. This proximity limited the independence of religious authority [PDF p. 48]. The Hippodrome provided a venue for chariot races [PDF p. 48]. Violent factions like the Blues and Greens emerged there [PDF p. 49].

Constantine chose Byzantium as his capital in 328 A.D. [PDF p. 54]. The city was consecrated on May 11, 330 A.D. [PDF p. 54]. He attracted diverse populations through corn distributions [PDF p. 55]. Constantine died on May 22, 337 A.D. [PDF p. 57]. His successors faced civil wars and barbarian threats [PDF p. 57]. The Goths settled peacefully near the Danube [PDF p. 60]. However, the Huns attacked the Goths around 372 A.D. [PDF p. 61]. This forced the Visigoths to request Roman entry [PDF p. 62]. Emperor Valens allowed them to settle as allies [PDF p. 63]. Corrupt officials then extorted food from the Goths [PDF p. 63]. Fritigern led a revolt against these officials [PDF p. 64]. Gothic cavalry proved decisive at the Battle of Adrianople [PDF p. 65, 66]. This defeat decimated the Eastern Roman legions [PDF p. 67]. Theodosius attempted to stabilize the empire through treaties [PDF p. 68]. He used Gothic war-bands as imperial auxiliaries [PDF p. 69]. The author argues this experiment was dangerous [PDF p. 69].

After 395 A.D., factional wars began [PDF p. 70]. The sons of Theodosius were both weak rulers [PDF p. 70]. Alaric led the Visigoths into Italy [PDF p. 75]. The Gothic leader Gainas fell in Constantinople [PDF p. 77]. Theodoric conquered Italy for Emperor Zeno in 488 A.D. [PDF p. 90]. The East survived because of its rulers' character [PDF p. 86]. Justinus took the throne in 518 A.D. [PDF p. 91]. His nephew Justinian became his successor [PDF p. 92]. Justinian married the influential Theodora in 526 A.D. [PDF p. 93]. He aimed to reconquer the lost Western provinces [PDF p. 99]. The general Belisarius proved to be a vital asset [PDF p. 100]. Justinian managed revenue through intense taxation [PDF p. 98, 99]. The author compares his greatness to Louis XIV [PDF p. 95].

Political factions included the Blues and the Greens [PDF p. 101]. The Blues supported Justinus and orthodox views [PDF p. 101]. The Greens supported Monophysitism and Emperor Anastasius [PDF p. 101]. Rioting erupted in 532 A.D. [PDF p. 102]. This sparked the violent six-day Nika riot [PDF p. 102]. Rebels attempted to install Hypatius as Caesar [PDF p. 102-103]. Empress Theodora refused to flee [PDF p. 105]. Belisarius eventually routed the rebels in the Hippodrome [PDF p. 105]. This massacre resulted in 35,000 deaths [PDF p. 106]. Belisarius defeated the Vandals in 533 A.D. [PDF p. 110]. He later captured the city of Ravenna [PDF p. 114-117]. King Baduila ruled Rome from 550 to 561 [PDF p. 120-121]. The general Narses defeated the Goths in 553 [PDF p. 121]. A great plague struck the empire in 542 [PDF p. 127]. It killed 5,000 people daily in Constantinople [PDF p. 127]. Justinian remained significant for his legal codes [PDF p. 132]. He also commissioned the architect Anthemius [PDF p. 133].
