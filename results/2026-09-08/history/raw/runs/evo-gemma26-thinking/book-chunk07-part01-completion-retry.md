The Angeli dynasty presided over a declining and disorganized empire [PDF p. 301]. Isaac preferred gorgeous raiment while Alexius preferred the table [PDF p. 301]. The empire suffered from severe military and financial instability [PDF p. 301]. Isaac lost Bulgaria and Cyprus through incompetent leadership [PDF p. 303]. Alexius III later usurped the throne and blinded Isaac [PDF p. 304].

In 1203, the Fourth Crusade arrived at Venice [PDF p. 305]. The Crusaders were unable to pay their Venetian debts [PDF p. 305]. They first attacked Zara to satisfy their financial obligations [PDF p. 306]. The young Alexius Angelus promised Byzantine wealth to redirect them [PDF p. 307]. The leaders were Dandolo, Boniface, and Baldwin [PDF p. 307]. Crusaders breached the sea-walls of Constantinople using light towers [PDF p. 310]. They finally entered the city during the 1204 siege [PDF p. 314]. The Crusaders then conducted a three-day sack of the city [PDF p. 316]. They killed thousands of unarmed citizens during the plunder [PDF p. 316]. They looted churches and melted ancient statues for money [PDF p. 316, 317]. Baldwin of Flanders was elected as the new Emperor [PDF p. 318]. The conquerors partitioned the empire among themselves and Venice [PDF p. 318].

The Latin Empire faced extreme instability and territorial loss [PDF p. 320]. Baldwin I died in 1205 after being killed by Bulgarians [PDF p. 321]. Greek resistance emerged in Nicaea under Theodore Lascaris [PDF p. 324]. Theodore successfully defended his realm against the Seljouk Sultan [PDF p. 325]. Other Greek states like the Despotate of Epirus also appeared [PDF p. 326]. John III of Nicaea was an excellent and worthy heir [PDF p. 327]. Michael Paleologus eventually recaptured Constantinople in 1261 [PDF p. 331]. However, the restored empire faced severe commercial and administrative decay [PDF p. 333, 336]. Michael Paleologus's decision to disarm native militias aided the Ottomans [PDF p. 339]. This policy left the eastern frontiers vulnerable to conquest [PDF p. 339]. The Ottoman leaders Othman and Orkhan successfully expanded their domain [PDF p. 349]. Orkhan established the Janissary corps using tribute children [PDF p. 350]. This corps was the first steady infantry for an Eastern power [PDF p. 350].
