C.W.C. Oman notes that earlier historians viewed Byzantium as decadent [13]. Finlay and Bury revised this perception [13]. Megarian colonists founded Byzantium at the Golden Horn [27]. They chose the site following an oracle from Delphi [29]. This location provided strategic military and commercial advantages [30]. Vespasian later stripped the city of Roman privileges [34].

Constantine required an effective administrative and military center [42]. He eventually selected the old Megarian city [43]. Legend suggests he traced the city limits with a spear [44]. The city plan included seven surrounding hills [44]. The Milion served as a central point for distances [45]. The imperial palace was situated in the southeast [45]. A large forum known as the Augustaeum sat centrally [45]. The Senate House was modeled to resemble Rome [47]. The Patriarch's Palace was located near the imperial residence [47]. This proximity limited the independence of religious authority [48]. The Hippodrome hosted chariot races and violent political factions [48, 49]. Constantine chose Byzantium as his capital in 328 A.D. [54]. The city was consecrated on May 11, 330 A.D. [54]. He used privileges and corn to attract diverse populations [55]. The name New Rome was used mainly in poetry [56].

Constantine died on May 22, 337 A.D. [57]. His successors faced civil wars and barbarian threats [57]. Goths had settled peacefully near the Danube [60]. Huns attacked the Goths around 372 A.D. [61]. Emperor Valens allowed the Visigoths to settle as subject-allies [63]. Corrupt officials then extorted food from the starving Goths [63]. Fritigern led a revolt against these corrupt officials [64]. Gothic cavalry proved decisive at the Battle of Adrianople [65, 66]. The resulting defeat decimated the Eastern Roman legions [67]. Theodosius attempted to stabilize the empire through peace treaties [68]. He enlisted Gothic war-bands to serve as imperial auxiliaries [69]. The author argues this experiment was dangerous for the empire [69]. Theodoric conquered Italy for Emperor Zeno in 488 A.D. [90].

Justinus took the throne in 518 A.D. [91]. His nephew Justinian married the actress Theodora in 526 A.D. [92, 93]. Theodora was an intelligent and highly influential councillor [94]. Justinian aimed to reconquer the lost Western provinces [99]. He fought an indecisive war with the Persians [100]. The general Belisarius proved to be a vital asset [100]. Justinian managed vast revenue through intense taxation [98, 99]. In 532, the violent six-day Nika riot erupted [102]. The mob demanded the removal of several officials [102]. Empress Theodora refused to flee the city [105]. Belisarius ultimately routed the rebels in the Hippodrome [105]. This massacre resulted in approximately 35,000 deaths [106]. Belisarius defeated the Vandals in 533 [110]. Justinian also targeted the Ostrogothic kingdom in Italy [108]. A great plague struck the empire in 542 [127]. This pestilence killed 5,000 people daily in Constantinople [127]. Justinian is noted for his legal codes and architecture [132, 138].

Successors of Justinian faced increasing chaos and invasions [140]. The Lombards invaded Italy in 568 [141-142]. Avars and Slavs attacked the Balkan frontiers [148]. In 602, the Danube army elected Phocas [153]. Phocas executed Maurice and his five sons [153]. Heraclius overthrew Phocas in 610 [156, 157]. Heraclius reclaimed the holy places by 628 [161, 164, 165]. The Saracen invasion struck the weakened Roman powers [185]. The empire reorganized into military districts called Themes [193, 194]. Constantine IV defended Constantinople from a massive Arab siege [196, 197]. Leo the Isaurian defended Constantinople against a Saracen siege [211].

Theodora ended the iconoclastic era in 842 [237]. Basil the Macedonian founded a stable dynasty [240]. His successors were literary emperors who focused on administration [241, 243].
