The Byzantine factions known as Blues and Greens grew politically powerful [PDF p. 101]. The Blues supported Justinus and maintained strictly orthodox views [PDF p. 101]. Conversely, the Greens supported the late Emperor Anastasius and Monophysitism [PDF p. 101]. In 532, rioting erupted after botched executions of several rioters [PDF p. 102]. This chaos sparked the violent six-day Nika riot [PDF p. 102]. The mob demanded the deposition of John of Cappadocia [PDF p. 102]. They also sought the removal of the prefect Eudemius [PDF p. 102]. Rebels also attempted to install Hypatius as the new Caesar [PDF p. 102-103]. Empress Theodora refused to flee and urged a final assault [PDF p. 105]. Belisarius ultimately routed the rebels in the Hippodrome [PDF p. 105]. This massacre resulted in approximately 35,000 deaths [PDF p. 106].

Justinian later sought to reconquer lost western imperial provinces [PDF p. 107]. Belisarius led an army to defeat the Vandals in 533 [PDF p. 110]. King Gelimer was unable to prevent this swift Vandal defeat [PDF p. 110-111]. Justinian then targeted the Ostrogothic kingdom in Italy [PDF p. 108]. Belisarius seized Sicily and later captured the city of Ravenna [PDF p. 114-117]. The Goths eventually rallied under a king named Baduila [PDF p. 118]. Baduila captured Rome in 550 and ruled for eleven years [PDF p. 120-121]. He was known for his personal courage and sense of justice [PDF p. 120-121]. However, the general Narses defeated the Goths in 553 [PDF p. 121]. This campaign left much of Italy wasted and depopulated [PDF p. 122].

The Persian War forced Justinian to spend immense gold [PDF p. 125]. Simultaneously, a great plague struck the empire in 542 [PDF p. 127]. This pestilence killed 5,000 people every day in Constantinople [PDF p. 127]. The disease spared no race, class, or specific region [PDF p. 128]. Justinian's health and organizational capacity declined after the plague [PDF p. 128]. The death of Theodora in 548 also diminished his energy [PDF p. 129]. He remains significant for his legal codes and architecture [PDF p. 132, 138]. He employed Tribonian to lead a commission for legal codification [PDF p. 138]. He also commissioned the architect Anthemius to rebuild St. Sophia [PDF p. 133].

The successors of Justinian faced increasing chaos and invasions [PDF p. 140]. The Lombards invaded Italy in 568 under King Alboin [PDF p. 141-142]. This migration left many Italian regions under Teutonic control [PDF p. 143]. In the Balkans, the Avars and Slavs attacked the frontiers [PDF p. 148]. The Avars primarily sought plunder through frequent raids [PDF p. 148]. Conversely, the Slavs aimed to establish permanent new homes [PDF p. 148]. These groups occasionally acted separately or in opposition to each other [PDF p. 150]. The papacy also gained power during these turbulent years [PDF p. 145].

Unresolved questions remain regarding the true motives of the factions [PDF p. 101]. The text also notes the unpredictable nature of the plague [PDF p. 128].
